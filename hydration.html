Hydration Decoded

Water first. Everything else has to earn its place.

Her daily number

The rule: half her body weight in pounds, in ounces of water per day. On days she practices, plays or sweats hard, add about 20%. Then it's not "drink water", it's a number she can count.

Today

Tap a jug each time she finishes one. The micro goals: fill it at school once so 1.5 to 2 jugs are done by 3pm, another between school and practice, one more by the end of practice. Goal hit, no thinking required.

The salt-pinch habit

In the car on the way to every game: a pinch of salt on the tongue, let it dissolve a few seconds, then plenty of water. Sodium is the main electrolyte she loses in sweat, and the salt helps her hold onto the water instead of running it straight through. Five seconds, and once it's a ritual they do it themselves.

Use a high-quality unrefined sea salt. Celtic is my favorite; it lives in a little bag in both my kids' sports bags.

Water or sports drink?

Water is the default. For a normal practice under an hour, water is all she needs, period. A sports or electrolyte drink earns its place when:

  • Practices or games run over an hour
  • Tournaments and multi-game days
  • Outdoor sports in the heat, when she's sweating hard

The one number on the label: skip the front, read the back, look at the sodium. If the sodium is near zero, it's water in a costume.

The pee check and the signs she's behind
  • Dark yellow? Behind on water. Catch up now, not at game time.
  • Light yellow? Right where she should be.
  • Always clear? Drinking more than she needs.

Dehydration shows up disguised: a headache at or after practice, fading early, cramping in the heat. When you see these, hydration and salt are the first two things to check, before you blame fitness, effort, or a bad day.

Muscle cramps: the checklist
  1. Water: has she been drinking all day, or just at practice?
  2. Salt: is she sweating hard without replacing sodium?
  3. What she ate before: fried or heavy food close to practice causes stomach and muscle misery.
  4. Enough carbs: an underfueled muscle is a cramping muscle.

Fix those four before anyone panics. If cramping keeps happening anyway, that's a conversation for her pediatrician.
