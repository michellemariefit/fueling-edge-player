The Recovery Window

The 30 minutes after decide how tomorrow feels.

Within 30 minutes: the recovery snack

30:00

Tap when she walks off the field. The clock runs even if you close the app.

What to grab

The formula is fast-digesting protein + simple carbs. The simple carbs refill her fuel stores fast, and the protein starts muscle repair right away.

  • Best: a protein smoothie. Protein powder blended with fruit. Kids drink it even when they're too tired to eat.
  • Next best: eggs with fruit. More whites than yolks.
  • No blender, on the go: chocolate milk · rotisserie chicken or deli meat with fruit · yogurt with granola · banana with peanut butter · cheese and crackers.

Grocery store trick: no chicken packed? Stop at any grocery store before games. First choice a rotisserie chicken. None ready? Deli turkey or roast beef from the counter. Protein solved in five minutes.

Within 2 hours: the real meal

The snack starts recovery. Dinner finishes it. Regular protein + complex carbs + veggies. Baked chicken with jasmine rice and a veggie. Pasta with ground turkey and salad. Taco bowls. Normal family food, doing elite-athlete work.

The part parents miss: sleep

Recovery is a chain with three links: the snack, the meal, and the night. The food gives her body the materials; sleep is when it uses them. And it's not just how long she sleeps, it's when.

Deep sleep is front-loaded. The first few hours of the night carry most of it, and that early deep sleep is when growth hormone releases, the repair-and-grow hormone. Her wake-up time is locked by school, so every hour past bedtime doesn't shift that window, it cuts it.

The house rule: in bed before 10pm. A great recovery snack followed by an 11pm bedtime on a school night is half a recovery. The chain ends with the next morning's breakfast.

On a random Tuesday, missing the window costs a little. On back-to-back days it costs a lot: tournament weekends, two-a-days, a practice the night before a game.
