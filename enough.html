Is She Eating Enough?

The module most parents don't know they need, and the one I most want you to read.

The double bill nobody talks about. A growing athlete's body is paying two bills at once: growth, and competitive training on top. The far more common problem in youth sports is kids training like athletes while eating like regular kids. An underfueled kid doesn't look underfueled. She looks tired. She looks moody. She looks like she lost her spark.

The signs to watch for

No single sign means anything by itself. It's the pattern, showing up and sticking around. Tick what you've been seeing.

The fix: add, never restrict

Quiet addition. You add fuel to the day she's already living.

  • Add the add-ons: nut butter on the toast, avocado on the plate, cheese on the eggs. Healthy fats add real fuel without adding volume she has to chew through.
  • Make her drinks count: milk, chocolate milk, or a smoothie with meals instead of water sometimes. 100% juice in the game window.
  • Add an evening snack: yogurt with granola, a rice cake with peanut butter, a bowl of healthy cereal with milk. It fuels overnight recovery and it's the easiest meal of the day to add.
  • My healthy treats are made for this window: the cookie, muffin and pancake recipes in the Kitchen Pack. A treat that's secretly a recovery snack.
  • Upgrade her smoothies invisibly: avocado, oatmeal, or Greek yogurt. She can't taste the difference.
  • Feed every 2 to 3 hours: the bridge-snack system. Small and steady beats big and rare.
  • Breakfast is non-negotiable, and some training days deserve two: real breakfast at home, second breakfast mid-morning.
  • Fuel during long sessions: anything over an hour gets simple carbs during.

What not to do (this part is the whole module)

  • No calorie counting, ever. Not by her, not by you, not "just to check."
  • No weight talk and no body talk. Not negative, and honestly, not much positive either. Her body is measured in what it can do.
  • Don't police the plate in front of her. Add options quietly. Make the good stuff appear. Kids eat what's there.
  • Watch your own food talk. Kids absorb every "I was so bad today." In this house, food is fuel, for everyone.

An athlete who eats a lot is an athlete doing it right. Say that out loud in your house.

The Mason Jar Mix

In a mason jar, mix equal parts hemp seeds, chia seeds, and flax meal, a quarter cup of each. Keep it by the stove. A few tablespoons go into anything saucy: chili, stews, meat sauce, meatballs, meatloaf, even smoothies. They don't taste like anything, and they add fiber, healthy fats, and extra protein. They will never know it's there.

When to talk to her doctor

This is education from a sports-nutrition and training perspective, not medical care. Bring her pediatrician in if you see persistent fatigue that adding fuel doesn't touch, irregular or missing periods, ongoing repeat injuries, or any changes in her eating habits or her relationship with food that worry you.

You're not overreacting by making that appointment. Catching this early is everything.
