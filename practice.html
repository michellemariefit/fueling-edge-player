Practice-Day Fueling

The after-school bridge snack is the highest-leverage change in this whole program.

The school day is the problem. Lunch at 11, practice at 4 or later. By the time she steps on the field her last real fuel was 5 or 6 hours ago. She's dragging, she's cranky in the car, the coach says she seemed off. It wasn't a bad day. It was an empty tank. Put fuel between school and practice, every single practice day.

What kind of day?

After practice, either schedule

Practice drains her fuel even when it doesn't look intense. A little something after helps her recover for tomorrow.

  • Fruit plus pretzels or another quick starch.
  • A smoothie is a great option, especially if she trained hard.
  • Practice ran long and hard? Add a protein. Deli meat or Chomps, or fast-digesting protein in a smoothie.

The Double-Batch Rule

Whatever I make for dinner, I double. We're a family of four, so instead of 4 chicken breasts I cook 8. Same recipe, same pans, same oven, same time in the kitchen. Doubling a recipe doesn't double the work.

The next day, everyone, including mom, has a hot healthy lunch or after-school meal already made: microwave and done. That late hot lunch before early practice? Already cooked. Send it to school in a thermos. No takeout, no standing in the kitchen at 3pm. I've run this for 20 years.

The rule under all of it. Practice is where fueling gets practiced. Every food, every timing, everything she'll eat on a game day gets tested on a practice day first. Nothing new on game day starts here.
