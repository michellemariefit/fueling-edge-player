The Game-Day Clock

Type in the game time. The whole day fills in with real times.

The same clock, every game. The power is the repetition. After a few games it runs itself.

The rules behind the clock

The carb rule. Complex carbs (pasta, rice, oatmeal, potatoes) are the slow fuel, so they belong at meals: the night before, breakfast, and the recovery meal. Simple carbs (fruit, applesauce, juice) and quick starches (pretzels, toast, plain bagels) digest fast, so they're the fuel for close to game time.

The protein rule. Regular protein takes real energy to digest, so it belongs at meals with time to spare, 1.5+ hours before playing. Closer than that, if she needs protein, it has to be fast-digesting: egg whites or protein powder in a smoothie.

The veggie rule. Veggies matter every day, but they slow digestion. From 90 minutes out until after the game, they sit on the bench.

The nothing-new rule. Game day is never the day to try a new food, bar or drink. Everything gets tested on a practice day first.

The not-hungry rule. Nerves and early mornings kill appetite, but her body still needs fuel. She eats something, even if it's small and simple.

Digestion takes energy. Anything heavy, fried, high-fat or high-fiber pulls blood toward her stomach exactly when her muscles need it. So as game time gets closer, the food gets simpler.
